Sample Fraction (matrix)

What sediment fraction each measurement was made on, and how sieved and bulk samples are told apart

Sediment can be analysed whole (a bulk grab) or after sieving to a grain-size fraction, and a trace-element value means something different depending on which. Three sources record this on each measurement in a matrix column: ICES-DOME, MUDAB, and 4Demon. Mareano and Vannmiljø have no matrix; for those the fraction has to come from the grain-size measurements themselves (a later page).

This page reads the raw matrix values, harmonises them into a common sieved/bulk class with a sieve cutoff in micrometres, and shows an important subtlety: within one sample the chemistry and the grain-size are usually measured on different fractions.

1. What the matrix codes mean

ICES-DOME and MUDAB use ICES MATRX codes of the form SED<mesh>, “sediment, the fraction finer than <mesh> micrometres” (SEDtot being the whole untreated sample). Meanings are from each pilot’s code_lookup table.

matrix Meaning Cutoff (um)
SEDtot Untreated total (whole sample) none (bulk)
SED2000 Finer than 2000 um 2000
SED1000 Finer than 1000 um 1000
SED500 Finer than 500 um 500
SED90 Finer than 90 um 90
SED63 Finer than 63 um 63
SED62 Finer than 62 um 62
SED20 Finer than 20 um 20
PK_default Placeholder, fraction not stated unspecified

4Demon records only FS (a sieved fraction) or US (unsieved), and keeps the actual cutoff in a separate fraction_range column (micrometres), which is carried through to the slim database.

matrix fraction_range Meaning
US 0-10000 Unsieved (whole sample)
FS 0-2000 Sieved finer than 2000 um
FS 0-500 Sieved finer than 500 um
FS 0-63 Sieved finer than 63 um
FS 0-37 Sieved finer than 37 um

There is no SED64 code in any source: the fine cutoff of interest sits at 62 to 63 micrometres (SED63, SED62, FS 0-63).

2. Harmonised sieved / bulk class

Every measurement is placed in one of three classes from its matrix (and, for 4Demon, its fraction_range):

Class Definition Codes
bulk Whole sample, or coarse sieving at or above 1000 um SEDtot, US, SED2000, SED1000, FS 0-2000
sieved Sieved to a fine fraction below 1000 um SED500, SED90, SED63, SED62, SED20, FS 0-500 / 0-63 / 0-37
unspecified No fraction stated PK_default, missing matrix
Note

Coarse sieving to <2 mm or <1 mm (SED2000, SED1000, FS 0-2000) is the routine step that removes gravel and shell before analysis, not a fine-fraction separation, so it is counted as bulk-equivalent here rather than as a sieved fraction.

5. The fraction differs between chemistry and grain-size

Within a single subsample the chemistry and the grain-size are usually not on the same fraction: grain-size is almost always reported on the whole sample (SEDtot), while chemistry is run on a sieved fine fraction. So there is no single “matrix per sample”; the fraction has to be read per measurement, and separately for chemistry versus grain-size.

Source Subsamples with both Same matrix Different matrix % different
ICES-DOME 10,125 2,421 7,704 76
MUDAB 5,287 4 5,283 100
